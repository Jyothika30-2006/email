# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

This is the [Contributor Covenant 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
condensed to the parts that do work in a repository this size. The linked text is the canonical one;
where they differ, the canonical version governs.

## Our pledge

We want working on this project to be safe enough that people bring the uncomfortable finding —
the "your threshold is wrong", the "this leaks the subject line", the first pull request from
someone who has never opened one. We pledge to make participation harassment-free for everyone,
regardless of experience, body, family, ethnicity, sex, gender, disability, nationality,
appearance, or sexuality.

## Examples of behaviour we want

* documenting the measurement rather than the aspiration — quoting a test count only after running pytest;
* saying "I don't know" about a limitation, and writing it in README §15 instead of hiding it;
* reviewing the argument, not the person; accepting that a maintainer's judgement on a forensic
  threshold can be wrong and revisable;
* focusing on what is best for the people who will *rely* on the output of this tool.

## Examples of behaviour we will not accept

* sexualised language or imagery, unwelcome attention, public or private harassment;
* doxxing, or sharing someone's mail, headers, or case output — including "just this one screenshot";
* treating a real person's email as test data without their informed authorisation;
* publishing a vulnerability disclosure as a drive-by issue to embarrass someone;
* trolling, insults, and any sustained disruption of discussions or contributions;
* pressure on a maintainer to merge, to release, or to answer faster than they can.

## Scope

This applies in project spaces (issues, pull requests, discussions, the sample corpus) and in
public spaces where someone is representing the project. Maintaining a repository is not a licence
to be unkind in it, and neither is being a user.

## Enforcement

Report conduct problems privately through the repository's
[Security tab → Report a vulnerability](../../security/advisories/new) and mark the title
`[conduct]`. Reports are read by the maintainer, kept as confidential as a one-person project can
keep them, and answered honestly — including "I am conflicted on this, here is someone else who
can look".

Consequences are proportionate: a clarifying comment, an apology requested, an edit or removal of a
contribution, a temporary block, and — for sustained or egregious conduct — a permanent ban from the
project spaces.
